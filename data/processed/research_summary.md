## Methodology summary
**1. Dataset.** Daily South Korea Top 50 playlist snapshots (`data/raw/Atlantic_South_Korea.csv`): date, position, song, artist, popularity, duration_ms, album_type, total_tracks, is_explicit, album_cover_url. The raw file is read-only; every derived table is generated separately into `data/processed/`. The unit of analysis is a *song* (normalised song + artist), and each continuous run on the chart is a *stint*.
**2. Data validation.** Dates are parsed with an explicit detected format (here dd-mm-yyyy) and never silently day/month-swapped. Text is Unicode-normalised and whitespace-collapsed; empty artists become 'Unknown Artist' (flagged). Rows are rejected and logged if date, rank (integer 1-50), popularity (0-100), duration (>0), total_tracks (>=1), explicit flag or song title is invalid. Album types outside single/album/compilation are kept as 'other'. `song_id` is a hash of the case-folded normalised **song + artist** so titles shared by different artists stay separate. Duration is converted to minutes.
**3. Snapshot resolution.** Each observed date is complete (50 unique entries), incomplete (<50) or over-full (>50). A date with more than 50 rows is treated as *stacked snapshots*: rows are split where rank resets in file order and the snapshot with the highest song-set overlap with the neighbouring complete days is kept (rank distance as tie-break); the alternative is set aside, logged in the resolution table and never deleted from the raw file. Remaining duplicate song/date pairs keep their best rank. Only complete snapshots are *valid snapshots* and can prove absence. Calendar gaps are listed and never invented.
**4. Re-entry definition.** For each song, consecutive appearances are compared. If at least **3 valid snapshot(s)** lie strictly between two appearances (observed absence) the later appearance starts a new *stint*: appears → leaves a valid snapshot → stays absent for the minimum number of valid observed snapshots → returns. Stint 0 is the first appearance; every later stint is a confirmed **re-entry**. Calendar dates with no snapshot are counted separately (`unobserved_calendar_days`) and can never create a re-entry. The threshold is adjustable in the sidebar: 1 counts every one-snapshot dropout (mostly rank 49-50 flicker); larger values keep only longer exits. Short dropouts shorter than the threshold stay inside the stint and are counted as `dropout_snapshots`.
**5. Momentum metrics.** Baseline rank = last rank before the exit (51 'off-chart' for first entries). **Rank jump** = baseline - entry rank. **Peak rank** = best rank in the stint; **peak improvement** = entry rank - peak rank. **Popularity lift** = peak popularity in the stint - popularity at the previous exit (undefined for first entries; `popularity_change_at_entry` uses the entry-day value). **Days to peak** = calendar days from entry to the first best rank; **snapshots to peak** counts snapshots. **Recovery speed** = (baseline - peak rank) / (days to peak + 1), so same-day recovery is defined. **Decay speed** = (exit rank - peak rank) / days from peak to exit (undefined if the peak is the last day). **Volatility** = standard deviation of day-to-day rank changes (needs >= 3 snapshots). Negative rank jumps and zero peak improvement are valid outcomes and are kept. Infinite values are converted to missing.
**6. Momentum score.** Each component (rank jump, peak improvement, popularity lift, recovery speed, retention snapshots) is converted to a percentile rank **within its stint type** (re-entries are ranked against re-entries; first entries are ranked separately because their baseline differs). Percentiles remove unit differences and outlier dominance. The score (0-100) is the weighted mean: rank jump 20%, peak improvement 20%, popularity lift 20%, recovery speed 15%, retention snapshots 25%. Weights are judgement-based: retention is slightly higher because it captures sustainability, the rest share the remainder. A component undefined for an event (e.g. popularity lift for a first entry) is dropped and the remaining weights re-normalised, so the event is not penalised. Because scores are percentile-based, 50 is the typical re-entry and the dataset-wide mean is ~50 by construction. Robustness: the Methodology page reports the rank correlation with equal weights and with 200 random weight vectors drawn around the documented weights. The score is an analytical composite, not an objective measure of fandom.
**7. Retention.** **Retention snapshots** = observed snapshots in which the song is listed during the stint. **Retention days** = calendar days from the comeback date to the last observed appearance, inclusive. Calendar gaps inside that span are not assumed to be presence (`unobserved_days_in_stint`), and the first observed absence after the stint (`exit_observed_date`) is recorded with its uncertainty (`exit_uncertainty_days`). Right-censored stints are flagged: their retention is a lower bound, not a complete lifetime.
**8. Fandom proxy.** Entities (song or artist) with at least one re-entry are scored on re-entry count, mean momentum, popularity lift, rank recovery, recovery speed, retention and comeback sharpness (share of re-entries landing in ranks 1-25). Components are percentile-ranked and weighted: reentry count 20%, momentum 25%, popularity lift 10%, rank recovery 10%, recovery speed 10%, retention 10%, sharpness 15%. The composite is shrunk toward the median entity by sample size, (n*score + k*median)/(n+k) with k=3 (songs) / 5 (artists), so one lucky comeback cannot outrank a long record, then min-max scaled to 0-100. Bands (Low/Moderate/High/Very High/Extreme) use the 30/60/80/95th percentiles. Sample size and a confidence label (Low n<3, Medium 3-9, High 10+) accompany every score. It is a **behavioural proxy from chart data, not fandom size, a fan count or actual fandom strength**.
**9. Content analysis.** Song-level attributes use each song's most common value. Re-entry rate = share of songs with >= 1 re-entry; because exposure windows differ, re-entries per 100 exposure days are also shown. Groups with fewer than 10 songs or 10 events are flagged as small samples. Findings are associations in observed data.
**10. Statistical tests.** Two-group comparisons of momentum/retention: Mann-Whitney U (two-sided, non-parametric) with Cliff's delta effect size (|d|<0.147 negligible, <0.33 small, <0.474 medium, else large). Re-entry rates: Fisher exact test. Multi-group comparisons (album size, duration quartiles): Kruskal-Wallis. Numeric relationships: Spearman rank correlation. Events from the same song are not independent, so p-values are indicative; tests are skipped when a group has fewer than 5 observations. Language is 'associated with', never 'causes'.
**11. Recommendation rules.** Rules use quantiles of the *selected* songs/artists (song rules need >= 2 re-entries, artist rules >= 3): high momentum + high retention (mean momentum >= P75 and mean retention days >= P75); high momentum + low retention (>= P75 and <= P40); multiple re-entries (>= max(3, P90)); low momentum + high frequency (count >= max(3, P75) and mean momentum <= median); high artist fandom proxy (>= P80 among artists with >= 3 events); high album-track momentum (only when album tracks' median momentum exceeds singles', reported with its significance). Each recommendation lists its evidence.
**12. ML methodology.** Experimental model of P(an exited song re-enters within 30 days). Samples are (weekly cutoff date, song with a confirmed exit at that date). Features use only data on or before the cutoff; the label looks only at the next 30 days. Train and test are split chronologically with an embargo equal to the horizon. Metrics: ROC-AUC with a song-clustered bootstrap interval, PR-AUC against the base rate, Brier score, top-decile lift, plus a trivial recency baseline. A model is labelled 'reliable' only if the AUC lower bound > 0.60 and PR-AUC > 1.2x base rate; even then it is a descriptive risk ranking. The core dashboard does not depend on it.
**13. Limitations.** See the limitations list below.

## Limitations
- The data are observed daily Top-50 playlist snapshots, i.e. observed chart behaviour only.
- The Fandom Intensity Proxy is a behavioural proxy; playlist position is not a direct measure of total fandom size.
- Correlation does not establish causation; content findings are associations.
- Missing calendar dates can affect time-based interpretation; they are never treated as exits or as presence.
- Popularity scores are platform/API-derived and may be updated with a lag or use opaque methodology.
- External causes (releases, tours, TV, promotions, social trends) are not observed in this dataset.
- A Top-50 cut-off censors information: songs just below rank 50 are invisible, and exits near rank 50 can be rank noise (hence the adjustable minimum-absence threshold).
- Songs on the chart at the start (left-censored) or still on it at the end (right-censored) have incomplete histories; retention for the latter is a lower bound.
- Momentum scores are relative (percentile-based within re-entries); 50 means a typical re-entry, not an absolute level.
- Events from the same song/artist are not independent; statistical p-values are indicative.
- One date (01-03-2025) held two stacked snapshots; the retained one was chosen by similarity to neighbouring days and the two were near-equal, so a few presence flags that day are uncertain.
- The re-entry forecast is experimental/descriptive and should not be read as a dependable prediction.
